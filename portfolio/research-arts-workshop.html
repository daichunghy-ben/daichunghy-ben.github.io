Participatory experience research · Australia

People return for what they learn to make.

This study treats an art workshop as a co-created service. The value is not only the finished canvas, but the curiosity, confidence, and attitude built while making it.

The one-minute insight

Value has to become an attitude before it becomes intent.

In the reported model, experience values did not automatically create intent. They shaped how people evaluated the workshop, and that evaluation carried the effect into the decision to participate.

Evidence boundary: the cross-sectional data measures intention; it does not prove actual repeat attendance.

What is worth designing

Beginner confidence matters more than surface-level fun.

Learning value was the strongest signal for attitude. For a workshop, that translates into three concrete jobs: show what guests will learn, reduce the fear of “I am not creative,” and make progress visible during the session.

  1. 01
    Show the process

    Use step-by-step examples, not only polished final art.

  2. 02
    Create a safe entry

    Make it clear that beginners get guidance, time to experiment, and permission to make mistakes.

  3. 03
    Recognize progress

    Feedback and light-touch display make personal effort feel meaningful.
