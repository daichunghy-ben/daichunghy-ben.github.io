Virtual influencers and purchase intention

A survey experiment tested whether the message or the virtual creator's appeal affected purchase intention among Vietnamese Generation Z consumers.

Explore the findings ↓Methods & sources ↗
Participants
244 valid responses
Design
Four versions of a post
Published
18 June 2025

Audience acceptance shaped intent

Human endorser preference linked to lower purchase intent.

Participants saw one of four simulated Instagram posts, varying message strength and creator appeal, then reported their willingness to buy.

What a brand could learn

Check audience acceptance before choosing a virtual creator; an appealing appearance may not be enough.

What the study does not establish

The study measured what people said they might buy after seeing a simulated post. It did not measure purchases or sales.

How the study was conducted

2 × 2 experiment · Consumer research · Moderation analysis · Elaboration Likelihood Model

  1. 1

    Change the message

    Show a stronger or weaker product argument.

  2. 2

    Change the creator cues

    Vary the creator’s appeal. The study also examines attractiveness and similarity of attitudes.

  3. 3

    Check audience preferences

    Preference for human endorsers, called speciesism in this study, is measured separately. It is not an assigned post version.
