A simulated social-feed experiment separates argument quality from peripheral appeal to see which cues actually move purchase intention.

What stood out

Peripheral appeal created movement. Skepticism set the ceiling.

In this study, social appeal, appearance, and attitude similarity related to purchase intention more consistently than argument quality. But for participants high in speciesism, persuasion remained lower across conditions.

The boundary to remember

Not every customer wants to be persuaded by a non-human persona.

Speciesism was tested as a moderator. When human-preference beliefs were stronger, persuasive cues could not fully unlock purchase intention. That makes transparent disclosure more than a footnote.

From result to decision

Three principles are more useful than a long implication list.

01

Design relatability

Use interests, language, and community values to create the feeling that “this persona gets me.”

02

Keep a reason to believe

Appeal opens the conversation; clear, consistent information supports longer-term trust.

03

Disclose the artificiality

Transparency respects skeptical audiences instead of trying to pass the persona off as fully human.
