EV choice experiment

A choice experiment with young consumers in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City examined how range, charging, network size and body design affect EV preferences.

Which trade-offs shape an EV choice?

Participants compared vehicle profiles with different features. Multinomial and mixed logit models were compared with a latent class model to examine differences between buyer groups.

Two ways to value an EV

The reported model separates a utility-focused group (65%) from a design-sensitive group (35%). Range has the highest marginal willingness-to-pay estimate in both groups. Design estimates are less certain.

Model specification +

WTP for a feature = −βfeature / βprice

Feature units and price coding determine the resulting WTP unit. Cross-group differences need their own uncertainty calculation.

Study plan +
Question
Can the estimated preferences predict choices for consumers outside the estimation sample?
Design & unit
Prediction benchmark using respondent-grouped validation, followed by an independently collected sample.
Measures & analysis
Compare choice probabilities and calibration. Preserve logit-based willingness-to-pay interpretation separately from tree predictions.
Data & sample
Requires the full task-level data and model code. Budget the external sample against the desired precision in held-out performance and subgroup estimates.
Decision gate
Keep the simpler model unless prediction improves consistently. A pricing experiment needs observed demand and a separate causal design.

Product positioning

The results can inform product positioning and the features emphasized for each group. They do not establish demand at a particular retail price.
