Transport policy · Hanoi

Motorbike phase-out policy

A policy choice study examined how Hanoi residents valued travel restrictions, public transport improvements and support for changing vehicles.

CIEMB 2025 manuscript

Explore findings ↓
Sample
320 respondents
Method
Discrete choice experiment
Context
Hanoi transport transition

What makes a transition acceptable?

Participants evaluated policy packages. The model estimated how restrictions and support measures changed their choices, with willingness to pay expressed in VND per month.

Restrictions and support pull in different directions

Restrictions on fossil-fuel vehicles carried negative willingness-to-pay estimates. Public transport improvements, reinvestment and an electric-motorcycle grant carried positive estimates.

Policy package design

The study supports comparing support measures alongside restrictions when discussing a transition package. The estimates describe stated policy preferences.

The model does not forecast traffic, compliance or the outcome of a particular regulation. The sequencing proposal remains a policy interpretation.

Model
The project materials report n = 320, ρ² = 0.28 and an alternative-specific constant of +1.621. The figure uses values from manuscript Tables 4 and 5.
Interpretation
Greater dependence on motorcycles changes how restrictions are valued. Income, environmental concern and trust also appear in the reported interaction analysis.
