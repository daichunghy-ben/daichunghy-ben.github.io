Transport policy · Hanoi

Motorbike phase-out policy

A policy choice study examined how Hanoi residents valued travel restrictions, public transport improvements and support for changing vehicles.

CIEMB 2025 manuscript

Explore findings ↓Model map ↓
Sample
320 respondents
Method
Discrete choice experiment
Context
Hanoi transport transition

What makes a transition acceptable?

Participants evaluated policy packages. The model estimated how restrictions and support measures changed their choices, with willingness to pay expressed in VND per month.

Restrictions and support pull in different directions

Restrictions on fossil-fuel vehicles carried negative willingness-to-pay estimates. Public transport improvements, reinvestment and an electric-motorcycle grant carried positive estimates.

How the analysis works

Choice attributes

Restriction, transport, financial support and governance vary within policy packages.

Preference variation

Mixed logit and reported interactions examine variation across respondents.

Trade-offs

WTP summarizes each attribute relative to cost; values are not a policy budget.

Model specification +

Utility = policy-attribute terms + respondent interactions + random component

An interaction changes how an attribute relates to preference. It is not an independent policy benefit to add to the package.

Study plan +
Question
Which support package remains acceptable across districts, and does it change observed travel behavior?
Design & unit
Preference replication followed by a district-aware pilot; the assignment and comparison design must be set before intervention.
Measures & analysis
Compare model stability and subgroup uncertainty, then estimate a separate pilot contrast on observed uptake or travel behavior.
Data & sample
Requires task data, district sampling information and new pilot observations. Plan for district clustering, subgroup precision and attrition.
Decision gate
Keep predicted preference and observed uptake separate. Policy-effect claims require a defensible comparison and assumptions.

Policy package design

The study supports comparing support measures alongside restrictions when discussing a transition package. The estimates describe stated policy preferences.

The model does not forecast traffic, compliance or the outcome of a particular regulation. The sequencing proposal remains a policy interpretation.

Methods & sources +
Model

Interpretation
Greater dependence on motorcycles changes how restrictions are valued. Income, environmental concern and trust also appear in the reported interaction analysis.
