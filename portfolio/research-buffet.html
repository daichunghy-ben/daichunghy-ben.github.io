Buffet menu choices

Two studies tested how the order of menu information affected intended calorie intake, perceived value and the choice of a higher-priced buffet tier.

Explore the findings ↓Methods & sources ↗
Study 1
257 respondents
Study 2
147 respondents
Design
Menu-order experiment

Healthier menu first

Premium-tier choice fell from 52.4% to 23.8%.

Changed the order of food options or price tiers, then compared planned intake and higher-priced choices.

What a restaurant could test

Test menu order while tracking both food choices and premium-tier demand; the two goals may conflict.

What the study does not establish

These were stated choices and planned intake. The studies did not measure food eaten or waste in an operating restaurant.

How the study was conducted

Experiment design · Ordinal logit · Mediation analysis

  1. 1

    Change the order

    Show food options or price tiers in different orders.

  2. 2

    Compare the choices

    An ordinal logit model examines the choice of higher-priced tiers in Study 2.

  3. 3

    Examine perceived value

    The analysis examines whether people’s sense of value helps explain the choice. The reported indirect effect is −0.339, CI [−0.56, −0.15].
