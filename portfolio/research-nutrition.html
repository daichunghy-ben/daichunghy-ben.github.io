Student nutrition · Vietnam

Student diet quality

A survey of 280 university students examined nutrition knowledge, physical activity and the foods eaten on the previous day.

FCBEM 2025 proceedings-linked paper

Explore findings ↓
Sample
280 students
Measures
CoNKS · IPAQ-SF · DQQ
Design
Cross-sectional survey

How does knowledge relate to daily food choices?

The study combined a 20-item nutrition knowledge scale, physical activity measures and a dietary questionnaire. Regression examined their associations with diet quality while accounting for age and sex.

Knowledge differed across topics

Students answered basic dietary questions more accurately than calorie questions. The reported diet scores also showed more foods to limit than protective food groups.

Campus food choices

The findings suggest specific topics for nutrition education and areas to examine in campus food provision. Physical activity was associated with better diet quality in the regression.

A cross-sectional survey identifies associations. It does not show that an education or activity programme caused better diets.

Methods & sources +
Instruments
Adapted CoNKS: 20 items. IPAQ-SF: activity in MET-minutes per week. DQQ: 29 food groups with food-diversity and Global Dietary Recommendations scores.
Regression
The reported model explains 53.8% of the variance in the diet-quality score (R² = 0.538). Physical activity, age and nutrition knowledge have positive adjusted associations; sex is not significant.
Source
Values were checked against the hosted FCBEM paper, including Tables 2 and 3. The results describe this student sample rather than all Vietnamese students.
