Student diet quality

Co-authored a study of how students’ nutrition knowledge and physical activity relate to their dietary choices.

Survey measures · Data analysis · Regression interpretation

Output

Knowledge-domain comparisons and an adjusted diet-quality model for the student sample.

FCBEM 2025 proceedings-linked paper

Sample
280 students
Measures
CoNKS · IPAQ-SF · DQQ
Design
Cross-sectional survey

How does knowledge relate to daily food choices?

The study combined a 20-item nutrition knowledge scale, physical activity measures and a dietary questionnaire. Regression examined their associations with diet quality while accounting for age and sex.

Knowledge differed across topics

Students answered basic dietary questions more accurately than calorie questions. The reported diet scores also showed more foods to limit than protective food groups.

How the analysis works

Knowledge

Adapted 20-item CoNKS; three domains use 7, 7 and 6 items.

Activity + diet

IPAQ-SF measures activity; DQQ covers 29 food groups.

Adjusted association

The paper also reports group comparisons and Spearman correlations. Regression adjusts for age, sex, knowledge and activity; R² = 0.538.

Model specification

GDR score = 6.9845 + 0.1284·age − 0.0129·male + 0.1102·knowledge + 0.0029·activity

Coefficients are in the units shown, not comparable standardized effects. The paper rounds the activity interval to [0.003, 0.003]; its precision cannot be recovered from the printed table.

Research methods

Regression separates adjusted associations from the descriptive knowledge scores.

CoNKS · 20 items

Nutrition knowledge

The adapted 20-item CoNKS scale measures correct nutrition-knowledge responses. Its score enters the regression alongside activity and the demographic covariates.

IPAQ-SF

Physical activity

Activity is expressed in MET-minutes per week. Its adjusted association is evaluated alongside knowledge, age and sex.

Covariates

Age + sex

The reported regression includes age and sex. Adjustment does not remove all possible confounding or establish time order.

Association model

Multiple regression

The reported model has R² = 0.538 in the student sample. This is explanatory fit in the supplied analysis, not a held-out prediction score.

DQQ outcome

Diet-quality score

Dietary responses provide the modeled outcome. Diversity, protective-food and food-to-limit scores remain distinct measures.

Proposed next study

Can prediction identify a useful intervention?

Build a small, auditable prediction benchmark before testing nutrition support.

Question
Can baseline measures predict later diet quality well enough to guide a support study?
Design & unit
Prospective participant-level study for prediction; a subsequent randomized support trial is a separate phase.
Measures & analysis
Retain validated scoring and timing, compare simple and nonlinear models, then assess future-outcome prediction.
Data & sample
Requires raw scale responses, repeated outcomes, consent and a data dictionary. Plan participant count against model complexity, outcome variance and attrition.
Decision gate
Advance only if prediction adds useful information over the baseline. Predictive importance does not identify an intervention effect.

Campus food choices

The findings suggest specific topics for nutrition education and areas to examine in campus food provision. Physical activity was associated with better diet quality in the regression.

A cross-sectional survey identifies associations. It does not show that an education or activity programme caused better diets.

Study notes & sources

Instruments
Adapted CoNKS: 20 items. IPAQ-SF: activity in MET-minutes per week. DQQ: 29 food groups with food-diversity and Global Dietary Recommendations scores.
Regression
The reported model explains 53.8% of the variance in the diet-quality score (R² = 0.538). Physical activity, age and nutrition knowledge have positive adjusted associations; sex is not significant.
Source
Values were checked against the hosted FCBEM paper, including Tables 2 and 3. The results describe this student sample rather than all Vietnamese students.
